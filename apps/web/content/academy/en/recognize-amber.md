## Start with appearance

Compare confirmed specimens in a museum or a trusted collection. Look at the range of colors, from pale yellow to reddish brown, and compare clear, cloudy and opaque examples. A rough piece may have a dull outer surface. Color, shine and an apparent insect inclusion are all clues with limited value.

## Consider lookalikes

Plastic and artificial resin can resemble amber. Copal is also derived from tree resin, and products made from bonded fragments can combine amber with other materials. One GIA case report even documents an object with both natural and manufactured insect inclusions.

## Preserve uncertain specimens

Photograph unfamiliar beach material where it lies. Leave suspicious objects in place and keep away from them. For a known safe specimen or a purchase that needs authentication, ask a gemologist about nondestructive examination. Heat and solvent tests can damage the piece and expose you to hazards.
