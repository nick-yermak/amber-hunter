## From resin to fossil

Trees produce resin, a sticky material that can seal damage. A drop may trap a tiny insect or plant fragment before it hardens. Under favorable burial conditions, resin can survive and change chemically over geological time. The resulting material is amber, an organic gem. Baltic amber is often called succinite.

## What can a piece tell you?

An inclusion can preserve details of life around the tree, from the hairs on a small insect to fragments of a leaf. Many pieces contain no obvious inclusion. Baltic amber may be transparent, cloudy or opaque, and a weathered surface can look different from a polished face.

Deposits containing old resin can be eroded and the pieces moved again by water. The place where a piece washes up records its most recent journey. It tells you little about where the original tree grew.
