## Wind builds waves over water

Wind transfers energy to the surface. Wind strength, the time it has been blowing, and fetch all influence the resulting waves. Fetch is the distance over water over which the wind acts. A single reading from a beach weather station gives only one part of the picture.

## The shoreline changes the result

As waves enter shallow water, the seabed changes their shape and they may break. Where waves arrive at an angle, they can drive currents along the beach. Nearby stretches of coast may face different directions and have different exposure, even when the regional wind is similar.

## Treat field patterns as hypotheses

A line of stranded seaweed and twigs shows where floating material accumulated. Finding drift debris does not establish that amber is present. To investigate a local pattern, record previous winds and waves, date, beach segment and observations, including visits with no finds. The idea that a particular wind direction or time after a storm favors an individual beach needs local evidence. Amber Index is an experimental score and does not express the probability of finding amber.
